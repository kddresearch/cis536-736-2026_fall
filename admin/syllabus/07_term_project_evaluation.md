# Appendix: Evaluation of Computer Graphics Systems, Baselines & Aesthetic Fidelity
**Formalizing quantitative metrics, native Unity 6 / Blender 4.2 profiling techniques, baseline parity, and structured aesthetic critique for Phase 2, Phase 3, and Phase 5 deliverables**

In computer graphics engineering, simply asserting that *"Scene B looks more realistic or runs smoother than Scene A"* is insufficient and incurs a major penalty on the 300-point Substance Rubric. Because visual quality, geometric accuracy, animation fidelity, and runtime throughput involve direct engineering trade-offs (e.g., frame time vs. ray-tracing bounces, VRAM footprint vs. splat count, or cloud cost vs. batch makespan), every architectural claim must be backed by **controlled baseline comparisons, taxonomy-appropriate quantitative metrics, and disciplined visual evidence.** Unlike statistical data-science courses, formal null-hypothesis significance testing is **omitted** from this guide (see Section 3 for the methodological rationale); instead, you are evaluated on experimental control, measurement validity, transparent trade-off curves, and honest aesthetic critique.

---

## 1. Defining Controlled Baselines & Reference Separation

Before collecting numbers or exporting renders, you must lock the experimental environment so that differences between your **Baseline** and **Proposed System** reflect your claimed graphics contribution rather than accidental viewport changes.

### 🛡️ Controlled Test-Scene Invariants
* **Paired Scene Parity:** Baseline and proposed runs MUST share the exact same camera spline or viewpoint list, output resolution (e.g., $1920 \times 1080$), lighting rig / HDRI exposure, fixed simulation step (`Time.fixedDeltaTime`), and deterministic random seeds. Vary *only* the claimed pipeline component (e.g., static mesh vs. Geometry Nodes, unoptimized shader vs. multi-pass PBR, CPU particles vs. GPU VFX Graph 17, or local Cycles vs. AWS Deadline Cloud).
* **Reference vs. Candidate Separation (Photorealism & Reconstruction):**
  * **Ground-Truth / High-Budget Reference:** High-sample offline path trace (e.g., Blender 4.2 Cycles at 512–1024 SPP with fixed ACES/AgX view transform), held-out COLMAP camera frames, or clean rest-pose / mocap animation clips.
  * **Evaluated Candidate:** Your real-time Unity 6 HDRP/URP render, reconstructed `gsplat`/Shap-E output, or runtime IK/Motion Matching controller evaluated on the identical camera/time stamps.
* **Color-Space & Format Parity:** When computing image-quality metrics (`PSNR`, `SSIM`, `LPIPS`), export both reference and candidate frames in matching color space and bit depth (uncompressed PNG for tone-mapped sRGB comparison, or linear 16/32-bit `EXR` prior to tone mapping). Never compare a gamma-encoded screenshot against a linear HDR buffer.

> **⚠️ Avoid the "Cherry-Picked Frame" & "Kitchen-Sink" Anti-Patterns**
> Do not report a single peak FPS reading captured while looking at an empty skybox, and do not dump unrelated metrics into an unanalyzed table. Profile across a complete camera trajectory (reporting mean, median, and 95th/99th percentile tail frame times) and choose strictly the metrics governing your **5×5×5 Taxonomy** intersection.

### Key Metric Definitions
* **Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR):** For an $H \times W$ image with peak signal value $\text{MAX}_I$ ($1.0$ for normalized float or $255$ for 8-bit) and Mean Squared Error $\text{MSE}$ between candidate $\hat{I}$ and reference $I$:
  $$\text{PSNR}(I, \hat{I}) = 10 \log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\text{MAX}_I^2}{\text{MSE}(I, \hat{I})}\right) \quad [\text{dB}]$$
* **Structural Similarity Index (SSIM):** Evaluates local luminance ($\mu$), contrast ($\sigma$), and structure ($\sigma_{xy}$) over sliding windows rather than independent pixel errors:
  $$\text{SSIM}(x, y) = \frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1)(2\sigma_{xy} + C_2)}{(\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2 + C_1)(\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + C_2)}$$
* **Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS):** Computes weighted $L_2$ distance between deep feature activations (e.g., AlexNet or VGG layers) of $I$ and $\hat{I}$; lower values indicate closer human-perceived visual similarity, catching blur and texture washout that PSNR misses.
* **Pose / IK Fidelity (MPJPE & End-Effector Error):** Over $J$ tracked joints (or end-effectors) and $T$ frames comparing predicted world position $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{t,j}$ against target/reference $\mathbf{p}_{t,j}$:
  $$\text{MPJPE} = \frac{1}{T J} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \sum_{j=1}^{J} \left\|\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{t,j} - \mathbf{p}_{t,j}\right\|_2$$
* **Parallel Speedup & Scaling Efficiency (Cloud Rendering):** For $p$ cloud worker nodes with single-node baseline time $T_1$ and $p$-node makespan $T_p$:
  $$S_p = \frac{T_1}{T_p}, \qquad E_p = \frac{S_p}{p}$$

## 3. Why Formal Statistical Significance Testing Is Omitted in CG Term Projects

Unlike tabular machine learning or NLP benchmarks—where evaluating hundreds of independent test examples yields well-powered $p$-values, paired bootstrap hypothesis tests, and Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate control—formal null-hypothesis significance testing is **not required and generally infeasible** in a one-semester Computer Graphics term project for four structural reasons:

1. **Prohibitive Cost per Truly Independent Sample ($n$ Collapse):** In CG, the true unit of independent replication is a distinct **3D scene, character rig, photogrammetry capture, or simulation domain**—not an individual pixel or rendered frame. Authoring, lighting, baking, and profiling a single complex scene can take tens of hours; with $n \in [1, 4]$ distinct test scenes, formal hypothesis tests lack statistical power and cannot meaningfully distinguish real effects from scene-specific idiosyncrasies.
2. **Temporal Autocorrelation & Pseudoreplication Across Frames:** Capturing 300 consecutive frames along a camera spline yields 300 frame-time measurements, *not* 300 independent i.i.d. samples. Consecutive frames view nearly identical geometry, shaders, and cache hierarchies. Feeding autocorrelated frames into a paired $t$-test or naive bootstrap treats pseudoreplicates as independent draws, artificially collapsing standard errors and producing misleading $p < 10^{-10}$ claims.
3. **Deterministic Algorithms with Near-Zero Stochastic Variance:** Standard rasterizers, fixed-seed Geometry Nodes, deterministic shader passes, and fixed-step physics solvers have virtually zero algorithmic variance across repeated runs on the same scene. Observed variance in Unity frame times is primarily operating-system scheduling noise or GPU thermal throttling rather than statistical sampling uncertainty from a population.
4. **Human-Subjects (IRB) Barriers for Perceptual Studies:** Statistically powered perceptual preference or readability evaluations (e.g., two-alternative forced-choice psychophysics requiring $N \ge 30$ naïve participants) require institutional IRB review/exemption, standardized display calibration, and recruitment timelines that exceed a single-semester capstone schedule.

### What You MUST Report Instead (The CG Empirical Standard)
* **Controlled Paired Comparisons:** Report baseline vs. proposed metrics on identical locked camera paths, resolutions, and seeds.
* **Distribution & Tail Summaries (Healy, Ch. 5–6):** For frame times, pose errors, and per-frame image metrics across trajectories, report **mean, median, interquartile range (IQR), and 95th/99th percentile tail latency** (e.g., `P95` / `P99` frame time to expose shader stutters or overdraw spikes) using faceted plots or aligned dot/interval charts rather than single numbers.
* **Scaling & Trade-Off Curves:** Plot explicit resource-vs.-quality frontiers (e.g., frame time vs. particle count $10^3 \rightarrow 10^5$, PSNR/LPIPS vs. Cycles SPP or `gsplat` iteration count, or AWS Deadline makespan vs. dollar cost).

## 4. Subjective Aesthetics & Perceptual Quality Assessment

Quantitative metrics do not capture everything that matters in computer graphics: an overly blurred render can achieve higher PSNR than a crisp render with minor sub-pixel edge shift, and a 120 FPS locomotion controller can still exhibit robotic stiffness or uncanny posture. Every Phase 5 Final Report must therefore include a **Structured Subjective Aesthetics Assessment**—explicitly anchored to visual evidence so it never degenerates into vague self-praise.

### Structured 5-Axis Aesthetic Critique Rubric
Evaluate your final rendered output (and contrast it directly against your naive baseline) across the five axes below:

| Aesthetic Axis | Guiding Diagnostic Questions | Concrete Failure Modes to Inspect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Composition, Framing & Visual Hierarchy** | Does the camera framing immediately guide the viewer's eye to the primary technical contribution? Are foreground, midground, and background separated cleanly? | Cluttered silhouettes, cropped hero assets, occluded data labels, or awkward camera clipping. |
| **2. Lighting, Color Harmony & Atmosphere** | Are light sources physically motivated (or intentionally stylized)? Does tone mapping (ACES/AgX) preserve highlight detail and shadow depth without muddying contrast? | Uncalibrated bloom washout, floating ungrounded objects lacking contact shadows/AO, or harsh color clashes. |
| **3. Materiality & Surface Believability** | Do surfaces respond plausibly to grazing and direct light? Are texture scales, roughness variation, and normal details coherent across DCC transfers? | Plastic/uniform roughness, visible UV seams or tiling repetition, inverted normal maps, or sRGB/linear mismatches. |
| **4. Motion Quality, Weight & Temporal Coherence** | Do animated characters, simulations, and dynamic camera passes feel physically grounded and temporally stable across consecutive frames? | Foot-sliding, IK snapping/popping, particle billboard sorting pops, GI light-leak flicker, or Gaussian splat "floater" shimmer. |
| **5. Perceptual Honesty (InfoVis & Hybrid Tracks)** | Do color palettes, scales, and annotations follow Healy (Ch. 1, 5–6) perceptual principles without distorting underlying data? | Rainbow colormaps introducing false boundaries, non-zero baselines, illegible UI Toolkit typography, or CVD-inaccessible palettes. |

### Mandatory Evidence Pairing Rules
1. **Matched Side-by-Side Crops:** Every aesthetic claim in `REPORT.pdf` must reference a paired figure (Baseline vs. Proposed at identical camera pose) accompanied by **zoomed inset crops** pointing to both visual improvements *and* at least one honest remaining visual artifact.
2. **Metric–Perception Reconciliation:** Explicitly discuss at least one case where your quantitative metrics and visual inspection either **agree** or **diverge** (e.g., explaining why a slight frame-time increase was justified by eliminating visible shadow acne, or why higher PSNR did not eliminate a temporal flickering artifact visible in motion).
3. **Exploratory Critique Boundary:** If teammates or classmates provide informal aesthetic feedback during Week 15/16 prep, label it strictly as an **informal studio critique**—never frame unapproved classroom feedback as a formal IRB human-subjects user study.
